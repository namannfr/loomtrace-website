Buyer-side inference of undeclared sourcing risk

See the sourcing risk hidden in the order book.

Loomtrace is developing buyer-side software to help apparel brands identify orders that may have been produced outside their declared factory, using the commercial records they already hold.

The problem

An approved factory does not always tell the whole story.

Apparel buyers manage supplier risk through the factory named on the purchase order. They audit it, rate it and publish it. When that factory is over-booked, squeezed on price or short on time, some work may move to another facility without the buyer knowing. That second site has not been audited.

Undeclared subcontracting

Orders may be transferred to facilities the buyer has not approved or assessed.

Exposure the buyer cannot see from its own approved-supplier list.

Capacity blind spots

A factory's recorded capacity may not look consistent with the volume or timing of its orders.

A clean audit can sit alongside output the site could not plausibly produce.

Fragmented evidence

Order records, shipping information, factory registries and audit reports sit in separate systems.

Reconciliation is manual, slow and rarely repeated across the supplier base.

Difficult investigations

Compliance teams need understandable reasons to prioritise an investigation and to document it.

Unexplained alerts get challenged, internally and by suppliers.

>1 in 3orders, in one published study
What published research shows

A study in Management Science examined more than 32,000 orders placed by over 30 apparel companies with more than 220 factories. It found that more than one third of orders were subcontracted without authorisation, and that the behaviour followed patterns visible in the buyer's own data, such as below-usual prices, lead times and product type.

These are the study's findings, not Loomtrace results. The study sample is not specific to Bangladesh, so how well its findings transfer to that corridor is something Loomtrace must test.

    What makes Loomtrace different

    A different way to examine supply-chain risk.

    Most supply-chain tools depend on what suppliers declare or what an auditor saw on the day. Loomtrace starts from the buyer's own commercial records to ask what may not have been declared. The science draws on peer-reviewed research; applying it as a commercial product for apparel sourcing is the step Loomtrace is taking.

    Initial product

    Buyer-side inference

    The proposed product analyses records the buyer already holds rather than depending entirely on supplier declarations. Because suppliers do not need to onboard, time to a first result is intended to be short.

    Initial product

    Factory identity and capacity

    Matching supplier names to real factories, across English and Bengali sources, and examining plausible capacity month by month are central to the design. Without a reliable factory identity, no order can be tied to real capacity.

    Initial product

    Explainable evidence

    Potential alerts are intended to show the signals behind them, and every review decision is kept as a dated record. Teams can then explain why a supplier was escalated and what supported that decision.

    Planned, not a current feature

    A network effect with multiple buyers

    A factory serves several brands, and each sees only its own orders. Loomtrace proposes a privacy-conscious way to understand a factory's combined load across participating buyers, with each buyer seeing a utilisation band and never another brand's orders or prices.

    The initial product

    One focused module: subcontracting-risk review for a single buyer

    • Buyer order history ingested and checked
    • Supplier records matched to factory identities, with human review of uncertain matches
    • Orders assessed against plausible capacity and known risk patterns
    • Alert queue with reasons, investigation record and exportable evidence pack
    The longer-term vision

    Capabilities that depend on evidence, partners and review

    • Pre-order view showing how price and lead-time terms could affect risk before an order is placed
    • Cross-buyer capacity view, subject to legal and privacy review and at least two participating buyers
    • Free feedback portal for factories, in Bengali and English
    • Further sourcing corridors, starting from where customers source most

    Who benefits

    Built for the people who carry the question.

    Each description below is an intended use. None of these outcomes has been demonstrated yet; the pilot stage exists to test them.

    Responsible use and trust

    Evidence that supports judgement. Never replaces it.

    Loomtrace would handle commercially sensitive data and produce signals that could affect supplier relationships. These safeguards are design intentions, built into the product plan from the start.

    Human review

    Potential risk signals are intended to be investigated by people before any conclusion is drawn.

    Explainability

    Each alert shows the reasons behind it, rather than presenting an unexplained score.

    Privacy-conscious design

    Each buyer's data is intended to be held separately and never shown to another brand. Worker-level data would stay with the factory; only aggregates would be used.

    Careful evidence handling

    Evidence packs are intended to support internal diligence. Loomtrace does not claim that an evidence pack satisfies any legal or regulatory requirement; that would need legal review.

    Non-accusatory communication

    Loomtrace flags anomalies for investigation. It does not label a factory as guilty or non-compliant on the basis of a software output, and it does not name factories publicly.

    What a Loomtrace alert is intended to mean

    • The buyer's records contain patterns worth a closer look
    • Here are the signals behind it, and a suggested next step
    • A reviewer decides what to do and records the outcome

    What it does not mean

    • That a supplier has subcontracted or broken any rule
    • That the buyer has met a legal or regulatory obligation
    • That detection is guaranteed, or that a factory with no alert is clean

    A complement, not a replacement.Loomtrace is intended to sit alongside existing audits, traceability platforms and due-diligence processes. A buyer keeps those tools and uses Loomtrace to test whether what they report is consistent with its own order data.

    Market context and why now

    The buying decisions are made in the UK. The production happens in Bangladesh.

    Loomtrace is starting with UK apparel retailers and brands that source from Bangladesh. That is where its future users sit, where public factory registries exist to match against, and where a single corridor keeps the data work manageable.

    Evidence-based due diligence is gaining attention

    Regulators and boards increasingly ask whether a company has tested its supply chain, not only collected supplier declarations. The summary below is dated and factual. It is not legal advice, and it separates rules in force from proposals.

    • 2015In force

      UK Modern Slavery Act 2015. Large UK businesses with turnover of £36 million or more publish annual modern slavery statements.

    • 30 Jun 2026Proposed

      Government amendments introduced. They would make statement content mandatory, require board approval and a director's declaration of accuracy, and introduce penalties of up to the greater of £1 million or 1% of turnover. The Bill is before Parliament and is not yet law.

    • 14 Dec 2027Adopted, applies from

      EU Forced Labour Regulation. Applies to every company placing goods on the EU market, with no size threshold, including UK retailers selling into the EU.

    • Jul 2029Narrowed

      EU corporate due diligence directive. Narrowed in 2026 to the largest companies and now due to apply from July 2029.

    Design partnership

    Help shape Loomtrace with your own order data.

    Loomtrace is looking for a first UK apparel retailer or brand to work with as a design partner. The intended approach is a blind test: you share order history for factories where you already know some outcomes, Loomtrace reviews the orders without seeing those outcomes, and the two are compared against success criteria agreed in writing beforehand.

    A strong fit usually has
    • A meaningful Bangladesh supplier base
    • 12 to 24 months of digital order records linked to factory identifiers
    • Experience of a capacity surprise, suspected subcontracting or an audit that did not match reality
    • One accountable owner in sourcing or ethical trade who will investigate what is raised

    Security documentation, a non-disclosure agreement and a data processing agreement would be agreed before any data moves. Factories, exporters, researchers and industry bodies are also welcome to get in touch.
